26/01470/FUL · Does this location offer a genuine choice of transport modes?

Are sites on Station Road, Claverdon, sustainable?

Policy TR3 asks whether a location offers residents "a genuine choice of transport modes". Claverdon has one bus out on a Monday and one return on a Wednesday, and a roughly two-hourly train with none on Sundays. The journey to the station, and to every service in the village, is along a road with a single footway on the far side, unlit, no wider than a metre anywhere and 55 to 70 centimetres for half of the 1.4 km to the station, beside 40 mph traffic, ending at a full-height kerb about 20 metres short of the station ramp. For a wheelchair, a mobility scooter or a pushchair it is not a route at all. This page sets out what was measured, and how inspectors have decided the same question since the Framework changed on 17 August.

2 · Measure it

What one metre looks like on this footway

Clear width was measured with a tape and a straight edge at each change of section, from the proposed crossing point to the station. The footway is no wider than about 1 metre anywhere. For around half the route, including beside 40 mph traffic, it is 0.55 to 0.70 metres. For 60 metres past the village hall, shop and surgery there is no footway at all.

Benchmarks: DfT Inclusive Mobility (2021); Warwickshire County Council Design Guide Part 2 (2.0 m minimum, 1.5 m where constrained, 1.0 m absolute minimum over no more than 6 m). They are standards for new footways; here they describe the route the applicant relies on.

Past the hall, shop and surgery: no footway at all

For 60 metres in the middle of the village, beside the facilities the applicant lists, there is no footway. Pedestrians walk in the carriageway or on the verge.

3 · What is at the end

The footway stops before the station does

The south-side footway ends against a building corner at a lay-by, about 20 metres short of the station ramp, with a full-height 125 mm kerb and no dropped kerb. A wheelchair or pushchair cannot leave the footway at that point.

The station itself is an unstaffed request stop with no car park. Its own notice board tells passengers there are no regular bus services and that the village is a 20 to 25 minute walk. The walk back from the station to the site entrance, without stopping, took 19 minutes.

4 · The road beside it

Fast traffic beside a narrow footway, and an unlit crossing to reach it

The only speeds on the file are the applicant's own, from a count in the 30 mph section beside the site: an 85th percentile of 39.9 mph eastbound, with about 83 per cent of drivers over the limit. On the 40 mph section, where the footway is narrowest and drivers approaching from the station have just come down from 50 mph, no speed has been measured by anyone.

The point is not highway safety. It is whether a footway 55 to 70 cm wide, with no verge between it and the carriageway, beside traffic at those speeds, is a route anyone will choose to walk or wheel. Inspectors have treated it that way: at Copthorne a mainly unlit road at 30 to 40 mph meant journeys on foot "would not be attractive to the majority of occupants"; at Wolvey a narrow footway beside a 50 mph road decided the case.

To reach the footway at all, a resident must first cross the road, at the dropped kerb the applicant proposes on the site frontage.

There is no street lighting on any part of the A4189 through Claverdon. WCC Highways records "no existing street lighting within the vicinity of the site". WCC's Road Safety Audit review, item 5.1, recommends "sufficient lighting … to remove the dark spot around the pedestrian crossing point". WCC Ecology's proposed condition requires the boundary hedgerows to be "not illuminated (as far as possible)". The Neighbourhood Plan's streetscape guidelines say "discourage street lighting". No document on the file reconciles the three, and no response to item 5.1 is on the file.

5 · The Government's own score

Bottom ten per cent in England and Wales

The Department for Transport's Connectivity Tool, which the Framework says "should be used" in assessing locations for development (TR3(2)), scores the 100 m square containing the site as follows. Scores are a percentage of the best-connected location in the country.

The tool "does not currently take into account the quality of walking, cycling or public transport routes. This includes aspects such as footway presence, footway width, surface type, and lighting." Scores "should always be contextually appraised using local knowledge / evidence".Department for Transport, Applying Connectivity Tool, section 6

The score assumes the footway is there and usable. Add what the survey measured and the position is worse than 26.

Every destination type scores in the bottom ten per cent for walking and for public transport. By car the same square reaches workplaces at an above-average level. That contrast is what TR3(1)(a) describes: a location that does not limit the need to travel by private car.

A neighbouring district has already acted on a score like this. On 18 September 2026 Wychavon refused permission in principle for up to five dwellings at Astwood Bank, citing a Connectivity Tool score of 23, "narrow, mostly unlit pedestrian routes" and a limited bus service, using the tool "alongside other relevant qualitative and quantitative evidence".

6 · The test

Whose judgement this is

For a Green Belt site to escape being inappropriate development under the grey belt route, all four limbs of policy GB7(1)(g) must be met. Limb (iii) requires "a sustainable location, with particular reference to policy TR3". TR3(1)(a) says the location "should limit the need to travel, particularly by private car, and offer a genuine choice of transport modes for residents and users".

That judgement belongs to the Council. Warwickshire County Council Highways assesses safety and capacity, TR3(1)(c), and has said in writing that wider sustainability is outside its remit. A "no objection" from Highways does not answer limb (iii).

TR4(1)(c) requires development to "meet the needs of disabled people, older people and children in relation to all modes of transport". The Council's duty under section 149 of the Equality Act 2010 is to have due regard to those needs. The survey is the evidence that duty applies to.

The bus service is one 510 journey out and back on Wednesdays and one 513 journey out on Mondays. The 511 does not serve Claverdon. The train is roughly two-hourly on weekdays, with no Sunday service. Every other journey from five detached houses here, to work, to a supermarket, to most healthcare, to secondary school, is by car.
